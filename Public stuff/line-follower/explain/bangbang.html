Bang bang

The simplest controller there is: two states, no in-between.

The signal every controller works from

The light sensor points down and sits over the edge of the line, so its reading depends on how far the car has drifted toward the line or away from it. On the Calibrate tab you measure the reading fully on the line (\(r_{\text{on}}\)) and fully off it (\(r_{\text{off}}\)). The target \(r^*\) is halfway between them, and the error rescales the distance from the target so that ±1 means fully on or fully off:

$$r^* = \tfrac{1}{2}\,(r_{\text{on}} + r_{\text{off}}) \qquad e = \dfrac{r^* - r}{\tfrac{1}{2}\,(r_{\text{off}} - r_{\text{on}})}$$

So \(e = +1\) means the sensor is well onto the line, \(e = -1\) well off it, and \(e = 0\) means it is exactly on the edge, where we want it. The controller's whole job is to steer so that \(e\) stays at zero. It does that by speeding one wheel up and slowing the other down by the same amount \(u\) (the steering) around the base speed \(B\):

$$v_L = B + u \qquad v_R = B - u$$

Following the right edge, a positive \(u\) turns the car away from the line; following the left edge the sign flips. The controllers differ only in how they decide \(u\) from the error.

The idea

If the sensor says “I'm on the line side of the edge”, steer hard one way. If it says “I'm on the floor side”, steer hard the other way. That's all. It is the same logic as a household thermostat: the heater is either fully on or fully off, and it flips whenever the temperature crosses the setting.

The curve shows what the sensor reads as the car drifts across the edge. The controller only asks one question: is the reading above or below the dashed target?

The rule

$$u = \begin{cases} +T & e > 0 \ \text{(on the line side)} \\ -T & e \le 0 \ \text{(floor side)} \end{cases}$$

\(T\) is the Turn setting. On the line side the left wheel runs at \(B + T\) and the right at \(B - T\); on the floor side it is the other way round. The car never drives straight: it zigzags back and forth across the edge, and on average it goes where the line goes.

What you will see on the plot

  • The motor lines are flat steps that flip between two values, never anything in between.
  • The reflection line wobbles around the target: that wobble is the zigzag. It never settles.
  • Faster base speed makes the wobble bigger, because each wrong-way stretch lasts longer in distance.

Why it zigzags

Two delays make it unavoidable. The sensor reading takes a moment to reach the page over Bluetooth, and the motor command takes a moment to get back. During that time the car keeps turning the old way, so it overshoots the edge before the controller can flip. Faster driving makes the overshoot worse. Bang-bang has no way to say “I'm nearly there, ease off” — that is what the next controller, PD, adds.

Where it is in the code

The Bang bang tab's code card is the whole controller: compute the goal, decide which side the reading is on, pick +turn or -turn, and apply it to the two wheels.
